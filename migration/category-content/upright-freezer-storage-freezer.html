Why is -18°C the ideal freezer temperature?

Food safety regulations recommend -18°C as the ideal temperature for frozen food. In the early days of modern freezing, -10°C was considered the safe temperature for storing frozen food. However, as we learned more about the nature and benefits of freezing, lower temperatures were recognised as being more suitable. In the late 1930s, the American Fruit and Vegetable Association recommended maintaining a storage temperature of 0°F (-17.8°C), largely because 0°F was a convenient round number. It was some time later that the rounded figure of -18°C became accepted as the standard frozen-food storage temperature in Europe. In 1964, the International Institute of Refrigeration recommended a minimum temperature of -18°C for frozen food. Both national and international committees agreed with this and firmly adopted the figure in standards and legislation. Then, based on the 1967 'Code of Practice for Frozen Foods', the EC Commission drew up its own Directive for quick-frozen foodstuffs and, in 1989, approved a minimum temperature of -18°C for the storage of frozen products. In short, lower temperatures slow down chemical reactions and the growth of micro-organisms. Researchers have found that, when stored at between -30°C and -18°C, the reaction rate for fruit and vegetables is several times slower. For sensitive substances in particular, this means their vitamin content deteriorates significantly faster at higher temperatures. After one year of storage at -12°C, the vitamin C content in vegetables is only around 20% of the vitamin C content of those stored for the same period at -18°C.
