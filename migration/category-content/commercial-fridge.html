How does a commercial fridge work?

Every commercial fridge and every domestic unit works on the same physical principle, the Second Law of Thermodynamics, which states that when two objects of different temperatures are brought together, heat will travel from the warmer to the colder. This principle is used to move heat from food stored in a commercial refrigerator into a refrigerant liquid. This liquid obeys another physical law, namely that when it expands and turns to gas, it cools, releasing the heat through the refrigerator unit's cooling system. The closed cycle of warming and cooling of the refrigerant keeps the food inside cold and protected, while the refrigerant liquid, originally Freon and now more commonly tetrafluoroethane, should last indefinitely if the system is properly maintained.

Things to have checked regularly include:

Moving parts such as fans get the most wear and therefore need regular checks. - Electrical connections. Even if your commercial refrigerator unit is working fine, it will be useless if the electrical supply fails. Make sure your fuse boxes, wiring and connections are safe and up to date. - Cleaning. Components such as the evaporator and condenser coils need regular cleaning to reduce the load on the compressor and minimise the risk of failure. Tips on how to maintain your commercial refrigerator for maximum efficiency

- Clean the inside and outside of the refrigerator with a damp cloth or mild washing-up liquid and water. - If you use chlorine bleach, apply it directly to surfaces with a clean sponge or cloth. Never mix chlorine bleach solution with other household cleaners, as this may create harmful fumes. - To prevent odours, place an open box of bicarbonate of soda in the refrigerator or freezer. Replace it every six months. - Do not put hot food in the refrigerator, as it will raise the temperature of other foods. Instead, store it in airtight containers in the kitchen until it cools to room temperature. Once cooled, refrigerate it quickly to stop bacteria from multiplying. - If the doors are left open, humidity will build up inside the refrigerator. Try to keep them closed as much as possible. - When you buy new packaged meat or poultry products, check that they are securely wrapped and that the packaging is not torn or damaged.
