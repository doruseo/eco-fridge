3 Differences between a Display Fridge and a Storage Fridge

It goes without saying that a commercial fridge is one of the most important pieces of catering equipment you can buy for your business. When choosing a commercial cooler for your café, you have a range of makes, models and sizes to choose from. You also have a decision to make over whether you need a solid storage fridge or a drinks display fridge model. Both types of commercial refrigeration have their advantages, but the one that works best for you depends on a few factors.  

Insulation and Cooling

Storage chillers and display fridge cabinets should be considered in terms of insulation. Storage fridge models such as the meat fridge or under counter fridge include more insulation than glass door fridges. A meat chiller keeps your raw foods fresh. As a general rule in a restaurant, you should store raw foods in storage fridges. These cabinets are also more energy efficient than display chillers. However, the contents are not easily visible, which means staff may stand in front of the fridge searching for something for some time. Upright display fridges such as the drinks fridge or mini-fridge are more commonly used for merchandising and display. That said, they can be used for storage too, so consider the factors below when making your purchase.  

Cleaning Needs

One important factor to consider is the cleaning needs of these two types of commercial fridge. Drinks chillers, because they feature a display at the front, can pick up smears and need to be cleaned more regularly. Storage chillers have no glass, and with their solid door design and tough stainless steel exterior, they still need external cleaning but do not show marks as obviously as a glass upright fridge. If the cooler is visible to customers, you may want to consider the cleanliness factor when deciding which type of door you want your fridge to have.

Display Benefits

Another point to consider when you buy a commercial fridge is presentation. Glass front fridges make it simple to see the items inside. They can also help you keep an eye on your stock without having to open the cooler to check the contents. That said, if you do not want your customers to see the contents of your commercial refrigerator, particularly if it is out in the open, you may want to keep anything unattractive out of sight. Quick, casual eateries and open kitchens may use a drinks chiller near the till to let customers easily see which bottled drinks are available, or to display prepared foods. You need to decide how much visibility you want, or do not want, for your new chiller. As you look to buy a commercial fridge for your business, consider how it will be used during a typical day. Is it opened frequently? Will it be for customers? How much time do you want to spend cleaning it? Answering these questions, guided by the information above, will help you make the right choice.
