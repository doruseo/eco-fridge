Protect your Commercial Freezer from sources of heat

In order to keep your food chilled to the ideal temperature, the compressor works harder when it is near sources of heat. Not only does this make your commercial freezer work harder, it is also a waste of energy and may even shorten the life of the appliance. When laying out your kitchen, try to keep your commercial freezer away from the hob, oven, dishwasher, or any other appliance that gives off significant amounts of heat. It is also wise to keep it out of direct sunlight to prevent it from working in overdrive.  

Clean your condenser coils

The condenser coils of your commercial freezer remove heat from the unit. If those coils on the back or underside of your freezer become dusty, it becomes difficult for them to work as well as they were designed to. If these coils are working twice as hard as they should be, it causes higher energy use that could lead to costly problems for your appliance down the line. To make sure your coils are properly cleaned, invest in an inexpensive coil brush to remove any dirt that has built up over time with ease. You don't need to clean the coils constantly, just enough to prevent build-up that affects the unit's performance. Keep the doors closed

As the cold air escapes from your freezer, it takes more energy to return it to its intended temperature. Where possible, choose what you need before opening the doors of your commercial freezer. Making your choices before opening the freezer helps you to control how often you open it, which in turn will save a good deal of energy.  

Maintaining the Commercial Freezer

  Auto-defrost Some commercial freezers have an automatic defrost cycle that typically runs twice a day and removes ice from the coils. The resulting water then drains into a pan at the base of the cabinet. Normally, the water should evaporate by itself, so you shouldn't have to empty the pan. However, you should check the pan and clean it with washing-up liquid to make sure it is not overfilling, becoming smelly, or growing mould. You also need to clean the dust from the condenser coils and cooling fan at least once a year. If you allow build-up to occur, your freezer will have to work harder to stay cold and use more energy. Unplug the commercial freezer, then use a brush and vacuum to remove the dirt. It is best not to use compressed air to blow away the dirt, as this will only pack the dust further into the cabinet, causing it to clog and settle into the fan.   Manual-defrost Some commercial freezer cabinets do not have an automatic defrost feature, so they require manual defrosting. When ice has built up along the walls, you will need to follow the same defrosting routine. First, remove all food from the freezer, then turn it off. Wait for the ice to melt on its own, then switch it back on. Finally, you can return the food once the temperature has fallen to 0°F.
