Bar Fridge Running Costs

Let us break this down, because it is always important to remember that a bar fridge can sometimes cost as much to run as a full-size fridge. As we discussed above, the smaller it is, the less energy efficient and therefore the more expensive it can be over the long term, while the larger it is, the more energy efficient it becomes, and you simply pay a higher price up front. These are important points to consider when buying a mini bar chiller. Running a mini-fridge each day, depending on your energy supplier and, of course, which bar fridge you choose, can cost roughly between £3 and £5, which is relatively inexpensive. The power rating of most mini-fridges ranges between 50W and 65W, so if you want to work out exactly how much the one you have chosen will cost to run, you can do a little maths. Every appliance has a specific number of watt-hours. To find the cost of running it per hour, divide the watt-hours of the mini-fridge by 1,000, then multiply that number by the cost per kilowatt-hour of electricity (which you can get from your energy provider). To work out the annual cost, multiply the cost per hour by the number of hours it runs (168 if running continuously) and then multiply that figure by the number of weeks in a year (52).
